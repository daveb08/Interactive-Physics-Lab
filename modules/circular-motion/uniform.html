Model

A puck slides on a frictionless horizontal table, tied by a light string to a pin at the centre. Seen from above, the only horizontal force is the string’s pull toward the pin. Gravity and the table’s normal force are vertical and cancel.

Key ideas

ALGEBRA
Period (time per lap)T = 2πr / v
Angular speedω = v / r = 2π / T
Centripetal accelerationa = v² / r
Net force neededF = m v² / r

The speed never changes, but the direction of the velocity does, so the puck is accelerating. The acceleration and the net force point toward the centre. Nothing pushes the puck outward: cut the string and see.

Show the calculus

Turn on to see the position as a vector r(t), and the velocity and acceleration as its derivatives, with live values from the run.

r(t) = r (cos ωt, sin ωt)

v(t) = dr/dt = rω (−sin ωt, cos ωt),  |v| = rω

a(t) = dv/dt = −ω² r(t),  |a| = ω² r = v² / r

Position r(t)
Velocity v(t)
r · v (perpendicular ⇔ 0)
Acceleration a(t)
−ω² r(t)

d(|r|²)/dt = 2 r · v = 0 keeps the radius fixed, and d(|v|²)/dt = 2 v · a = 0 keeps the speed fixed: the acceleration only turns the velocity. On the graphs, the slope of x(t) is vₓ(t), and x and y are sinusoids a quarter period apart.
