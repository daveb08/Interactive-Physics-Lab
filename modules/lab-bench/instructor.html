How the four checks work

Value: the reported g is within the tolerance of the student’s true g; the tolerance depends on the timer the result came from.

Uncertainty: what a perfect experiment would measure (the true g, lowered by air drag on that planet for that ball and those heights) lies within 2 × the reported uncertainty, and the uncertainty is no more than the allowed percentage of g.

Method: the required number of heights each have the required number of drops, with one ball and one timer.

Explanation: the student’s first answer to the “Explain your result” question, which is chosen from their own data, is correct.

A code is refused if it was edited (checksum), damaged in copying, or made for a different assignment. If a student pastes more than one code, the last one counts. Identical data under two IDs is flagged. The checksum stops casual editing, not a determined student who reads the page source.

Grades are computed in this browser. Nothing is stored or sent.
