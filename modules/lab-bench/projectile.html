4 · Velocities

04

Mark the ball on at least 4 frames: each pair of neighbouring positions gives one velocity.

Work out the first three vx and vy values yourself. Each velocity belongs to an interval between two neighbouring positions: vx = Δx / Δt and vy = Δy / Δt, plotted at the middle time tmid. Type them in the velocity table, then check. As the columns fill in, compare them: what does each velocity do?

5 · Graphs and fits

05

For each graph, choose the model the data should follow and fit it. The fit gives the coefficients with their uncertainties; working out g from them is up to you.

How do the coefficients relate to g?

Without air, a projectile’s horizontal velocity is constant and its vertical acceleration is −g. Ask yourself which graphs should be straight lines, which a parabola, and which coefficient carries the acceleration. What should the slope of the vx–t line be, and what does that say about the horizontal force? If a graph is a parabola y = A + B t + C t², what is d²y/dt²? If a graph of vy against t is a straight line, what does its slope mean?

The ± on each coefficient comes from how far your points scatter about the fit. If you work out g from a coefficient by multiplying it by a number, multiply its ± by the same number.
